# Korean Capital Gains Tax Research

## 1. Research Objective

This document records official Korean tax sources relevant to the capital gains tax analysis of Hillstate and Jungang Heights.

The purpose is to identify the rules that must be verified before comparing the two property sale strategies.

## 2. Official Sources

### Source 1: National Tax Service — Capital Gains Tax Calculation Guide

- Website: https://nts.go.kr/nts/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?cntntsId=8799&mi=12271
- Research focus:
  - Calculation of capital gains using transaction values and eligible expenses.
  - Special calculation rules for qualifying high-value, single-family-home sales.
  - The KRW 1.2 billion threshold used in the relevant high-value, single-family-home calculation.

**Application to this project:** The single-family-home calculation must not automatically be applied to either property while the household owns multiple homes. Eligibility must be verified first.

## Source 2: National Tax Service — Capital Gains Tax Guidance

URL: https://www.nts.go.kr/

Purpose: Additional official guidance on capital gains tax rules, filing, deductions, and relevant exceptions.

**Application to this project:** Verify the version of Article 104 effective on each hypothetical sale date and determine whether the relevant multi-home provisions apply.

## 3. Key Legal Questions

1. What tax rules apply to each property on the assumed sale date?
2. Does the multi-home capital gains tax surcharge apply to the first sale?
3. How does the first sale affect the tax treatment of the second sale?
4. Is the long-term holding special deduction available?
5. Are any exemptions, transitional provisions, or temporary relief measures applicable?
6. How should local income tax be included in the comparison?
7. Does the taxpayer's Korean tax residency status affect the analysis?

## 4. Research Method

For each legal question:

1. Locate the relevant official legislation or National Tax Service guidance.
2. Record the applicable provision and effective date.
3. Document the assumptions used in the calculator.
4. Compare the calculator's treatment with the official rule.
5. Record any unresolved questions before accepting the estimate.
